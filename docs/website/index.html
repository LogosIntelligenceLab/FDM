What if actions
came before the future?

Future world states are a rich learning signal. FDM uses them to train action features, while keeping predicted futures out of the action path.

VLA

Predict actions from observations and language, without explicit future-state supervision.

Future-conditioned WAM

Generate futures to predict actions. Visual prediction errors can affect the policy.

FDM Our approach

Predict futures from action features. Learn from future states; deploy with action-only inference.

Future errors stay
out of the action path.

At perturbation strength α = 1.0, FastWAM-Joint’s action error increases by 4.44% on LIBERO and 5.52% on RoboTwin. FDM’s actions remain unchanged when model parameters, inputs and action-sampling randomness are fixed.

The FDM zero line is an architectural property under fixed inputs and randomness, rather than a fitted experimental curve.

Future gradients support
action learning.

The paper measures cosine similarity between action-imitation gradients and future-loss gradients through the action-feature interface. Alignment is predominantly positive for VLM and positive in most VGM settings after initial fluctuations.

The controlled ablations above isolate the behavioral contribution: full FDM improves average success over stop-gradient by 7 points for VLM and 5 points for VGM.

Experimental setup

Two ARX-X5 arms observe the workspace from three viewpoints. Demonstrations are collected through VR teleoperation.

Robot
Dual-arm ARX-X5
Workspace view
One RealSense D435
Wrist views
Two RealSense D405 cameras, one per arm
Teleoperation
PICO 4 Ultra VR headset
Control
30 Hz · 20-dimensional bimanual actions: position, rotation and gripper commands for each arm

Demonstrations

150 trajectories per task: 147 for training and 3 for offline validation. Object placements vary across demonstrations and evaluation trials.

Evaluation

20 trials per method per task. Human evaluators are blinded to model identity and judge whether the task goal is met. Timeouts and trials judged unable to continue count as failures.

Hardware, collection protocol and task definitions: Appendix D of the paper.

Five questions from
the experiments.

The main findings, in brief. Each answer links back to the evidence.

Does learning to predict the future improve task success?

It helps across simulation and real-robot tests. On the five real tasks, FDM improves average success from 83% to 96% for the VLM family and from 78% to 83% over FastWAM-Joint for the VGM family. The gains are not universal: on LIBERO, FDM w/ VGM reaches 98.1%, slightly below LingBot-VA’s 98.5%.

Explore task success →
Why send the future loss back through action features?

Those gradients make the action features more useful for control. Compared with keeping the same forward connection but stopping its gradients, full FDM gains 7 percentage points for VLM and 5 for VGM on the real tasks.

Compare the ablations →
Can a wrong future prediction change the action?

In FDM, actions never read predicted futures, so changing a future prediction leaves actions unchanged when model parameters, inputs and sampling randomness are fixed. FastWAM-Joint depends on those futures: at the strongest tested perturbation, its action error rises by 4.44% on LIBERO and 5.52% on RoboTwin.

See the perturbation analysis →
Do future supervision and action imitation work together?

The measured gradients generally point in compatible directions. Their alignment is mostly positive for VLM and becomes positive in most VGM settings after early fluctuations, supporting future prediction as a useful training signal for action learning.

See the gradient analysis →
What does FDM cost to train and run?

For VLM, training steps take about 42% longer, while inference stays close to π₀.₅ at about 203 ms per action chunk. For VGM, training cost is nearly unchanged and inference drops from 446.55 to 253.45 ms per chunk, about 1.8× faster than FastWAM-Joint on one H100.
